# CAD reference research — 2026-10-07

This is a comparative research note for the Apex-2 cage, not a change to the project's upstream reference rule. **The only upstream design for this project is kert's Thingiverse 408519**, supplied by the user as two ZIP files. Other projects below are secondary technical references; none is an approved source model or a dimension source for the Apex-2.

## Recommendation

Use the preserved Thingiverse package for the starting cage topology and segmented two-ring arrangement. Study the separate gyroscopic-guard project for its SolidWorks assembly organization and spherical cage/gimbal integration, but do not copy its files without permission: the repository has no declared license and its aircraft/cage are much larger. Use OpenGimbal only to study bearing blocks and pivot stacks; it is a bench test rig under GPL-3.0, not an in-flight cage. CogniFly is useful for rod-to-printed-joint retention and compliant crash parts, but it has no declared license and is not a gimballed sphere.

No one located is a drop-in, licensed Apex-2 model. Model a measured fixed-cage P1A around the actual aircraft first; reserve common attachment interfaces for the removable P1B rings. Rebuild every hardware interface and validate the full swept prop, wire, camera and airflow envelopes.

## Structural question: is 1 mm rod enough?

**Not established for the rods in the BOM.** The original Thingiverse design calls for 3 mm rods. The current BOM's [VictorsHome 1 mm × 400 mm rod listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BDL6X2ZG?th=1) states material and dimensions but gives no engineering datasheet, fiber orientation, tensile/compressive rating, modulus or tolerances. A separate supplier's [1 mm pultruded unidirectional rod](https://www.easycomposites.co.uk/1mm-carbon-fibre-rod) lists typical lengthwise tensile strength of 400–500 MPa and modulus of 28–40 GPa. If that exact product were a perfect 1 mm solid section, its nominal axial tensile failure load would be about 314–393 N (strength × 0.785 mm² area). Those typical values do **not** establish the strength of the ordered VictorsHome rods, or the strength of a rod/hub assembly under impact.

For scale only, use the supplier's typical lengthwise modulus for its own pultruded unidirectional rods. An ideal straight, pin-ended Euler column at a 66.5 mm unsupported span gives approximate elastic buckling loads of **3.1–4.4 N at 1 mm**, **15.5–22.2 N at 1.5 mm**, and **248–355 N at 3 mm**. The assumed 66.5 mm span comes from the Thingiverse README. These are idealized calculations, not ratings for the ordered product, not a full-cage model, and not impact allowables; actual joints, rod imperfections, fiber construction, end restraint and cage load paths can move the results substantially. The comparison shows why a high axial tensile claim alone cannot establish suitability: for a solid round member, bending stiffness and Euler buckling capacity scale with diameter to the fourth power when other assumptions are held equal.

Keep the user's 1 mm rule as a **prototype starting point**, not a validated production choice. Before a full cage print, test a representative member length, hub, connector and mounting condition. Record rod diameter/lot, unsupported span, load direction and magnitude, load duration, loaded and residual deflection, socket pullout, joint slip and post-test damage. Set the allowable deflection from the actual prop-clearance budget and test with the heaviest battery configuration being considered; OVONIC's 1100 mAh listing claims 65 g per pack, but its fields are inconsistent and the actual pack must be weighed. If a representative 1 mm member fails its measured clearance/retention gate, revise geometry or reinforce only the tested weak locations; do not treat 1.5 mm as proven merely because it is larger.

## CAD workflow suggested by the evidence

1. Keep the untouched Thingiverse ZIPs in `cad/references/`; create new native project geometry in a separate directory with source, units, version and revision recorded.
2. Measure the frame, motor centers, installed props, camera, battery, rods and actual bearings/pins. The 95 mm wheelbase is not the full prop or aircraft envelope.
3. Build the aircraft envelope and a fixed-cage P1A carrier first. Keep quad and battery X/Y adjustment and the strap-style battery mount.
4. Parameterize outer span, member diameter/length, hub sockets, segmented hoop dimensions and replaceable mounts. Do not uniformly scale hardware holes.
5. Add P1B as removable rings on the shared interface after the fixed-cage gate. Sweep all reachable poses against full prop disks, camera view, wiring and airflow; zero intrusion is allowed.
6. Track P1A/P1B estimated mass separately, then replace estimates with slicer and scale measurements. Print a representative rod/joint section before a full batch.

This review found useful design references, but no outside project removes the need for measured Apex-2 dimensions, fit testing, the ten-contact P1A gate, or an original parametric model.
